What's inside the Unitree Go2

Every wireless product sold in the US has to be tested by the FCC, and after launch the test lab's photos of the opened product become public. Unitree's Go2 robot dog was taken apart for 68 pages. We read every page and named what could be named — and marked what Unitree deliberately made unreadable.

How it is wired

Read straight off the silkscreen of the main board, “712 MAIN BOARD-RK V0.21, 2023/6/17”. Every label below is printed on the board itself.

Every part, and how sure we are

Read means the name is printed on the part and legible in the photo. Matched means it is not legible on the Go2 but is identified another way, explained on the card.

The brain: Rockchip RK3588S core board Matched

A plug-in computer board, XF_RK_Core_Board_V10, sits on the main board under an aluminium heatsink with copper heat pipes and a blower fan. It holds the main processor, its memory, its storage and a power-management chip. On the Go2 every chip top has been ground blank, but the board is silkscreened “8+64G”, i.e. 8 GB of memory and 64 GB of storage.

How we know it is an RK3588S: the identical board, with the same name, layout and “8+64G” label, appears in Unitree's 2025 humanoid filing (FCC ID 2A5PE-YUSHU008) with its markings intact: Rockchip RK3588S, two Biwin BWMZCX32H2A-32G-X memory chips, Biwin BWCTAKJ11X64G 64 GB storage and a Rockchip power-management chip. So the Go2 and the humanoid share one brain.

  • RK3588S: 4× Cortex-A76 + 4× Cortex-A55, 6 TOPS neural accelerator, built for cameras and on-device AI.
  • The Go2 EDU can add an NVIDIA Jetson on top; the unit in this filing is the standard computer.

4G: Quectel EG25-G module Read

A standard M.2 cellular card, labelled on its shield with FCC ID XMR201903EG25G. The lab opened the shield: inside are a Qualcomm MDM9207 modem, Nanya NM1282KSLAXAL-3B memory, a Qualcomm WTR2965 radio transceiver and two Qorvo power amplifiers (77031, 77033).

Why it is there. It lets the dog be reached from the phone app when it is far from home Wi-Fi.

Pages 5–7

Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth: Realtek RTL8852BU Read

A soldered-down module, labelled BL-M8852BU1 (an LB-Link part), carries the Realtek RTL8852BU: dual-band Wi-Fi 6 plus Bluetooth over USB. Two antenna cables, marked “Wi-Fi chain 0” and “chain 1”, go to Rainsun antennas. The phone app and controller connect here.

Pages 2, 3, 10, 41

Remote-control radio board “YS.PPRF” Partly read

A separate radio board, silkscreened YS.PPRF 23, with a 2401C 2.4 GHz front-end amplifier and a crystal. Its two main chips have had their markings ground off. A separate LoRa antenna cable is also listed, so the dog has a long-range, low-bandwidth link in addition to Wi-Fi.

Pages 9, 42

UWB positioning board Chips hidden

A gold-coloured board with four UWB antennas, the hardware behind the Go2's “follow me” mode: ultra-wideband radio measures the exact distance and angle to the handheld remote. The lab labels the main chip “UWB chip”. Unitree ground its markings off, along with the amplifiers beside it.

Pages 35–38

Twelve leg motors, each with its own driver Read

Each leg has three joints (hip side-swing, hip, knee), and every joint is a flat outrunner brushless motor. A round driver board, silkscreened “M12_MDRV_V1.9.1 · 2023/5/10 · Unitree”, sits inside the joint with its power transistors, capacitors and an 8-pin chip in the centre. A small star-shaped board, most likely the position sensor, sits at the centre of the joint. The main board only sends commands down one bus per leg (FR, FL, RL, RR); the high current never leaves the joint.

  • The lab opened every leg: stator windings, magnet rings and the driver boards are all photographed.
  • The centre chip on the driver board has had its markings removed.

Pages 11–31, 48–68

A second small motor board, “LD BOT V0.2.6” Board read, role inferred

A compact brushless motor, about 5 cm across, paired with a board marked “LD BOT V0.2.6 By Unitree”. It is far smaller than the leg motors. The main board has a port labelled LIDAR, and the Go2 carries a spinning 4D LiDAR in its head, so this is most likely the motor that spins it. That is an inference; the filing does not say.

Pages 23–28

Cooling: two 12 V blower fans Read

Small DC brushless blowers from Shenzhen Zhuoming Electronics, 12 V. One sits beside the core board's heatsink; a second one is in the body.

Pages 2, 4, 5, 45

What Unitree hides

On four boards, the processor, the motor-driver chip, the UWB chip and the remote radio's chips, the printed part numbers have been physically ground off before the robot went to the lab. Everything bought in as a finished module (the 4G card, the Wi-Fi module) was left readable. It is a common way to slow down copycats, and it is why the processor above is identified by matching boards rather than read directly.

Questions

What processor does the Unitree Go2 use?

A Rockchip RK3588S, an 8-core Arm chip with a built-in AI accelerator, on a plug-in core board marked XF_RK_Core_Board_V10 with 8 GB of memory and 64 GB of storage. The chip tops on the Go2 are ground blank, but the same core board appears in Unitree's 2025 humanoid filing with its markings intact.

Does the Unitree Go2 have 4G?

The unit Unitree sent for FCC testing has a Quectel EG25-G 4G LTE module (FCC ID XMR201903EG25G) built on a Qualcomm MDM9207 modem, plugged into an M.2 slot on the main board. It also carries Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth (Realtek RTL8852BU), UWB and a separate long-range radio for the remote.

How many motors does the Go2 have?

Twelve leg motors, three per leg. Each is an outrunner brushless motor with its own round driver board (marked M12_MDRV_V1.9.1, dated 2023/5/10) mounted inside the joint, so the main computer sends commands over a bus instead of driving motor current itself.
